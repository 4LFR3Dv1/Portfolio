# R0.2-PRE — Brine Identity Grounding

Status: **RESEARCH GROUNDING / NON-NORMATIVE**

Portfolio baseline: `main@3147dac4a31da58bdf800dccdc0851ca31495d74`

This cut is a research bridge between the frozen Editorial Constitution (R0.1) and the normative Record Identity Contract (R0.2). It does not define the final Record schema. It extracts identity laws from prior BrineOS continuity research and translates only the implementation-independent parts that are relevant to an editorial knowledge system.

## Public/private boundary

The grounding source is an internal BrineOS research repository. This public document intentionally does **not** copy private repository URLs, private implementation locators, physical target identifiers, network identifiers, artifact hashes or unpublished evidence payloads into Portfolio.

The public result is the derived identity model, not disclosure of the private source corpus. This is an application of EC-13: private knowledge cannot leak by composition.

## Research question

> What must remain invariant for one editorial Record to remain the same Record while its state, revision, representation, route, storage realization or interpretation changes?

The grounding studies nine Brine research boundaries:

| Source boundary | Identity property extracted for R0.2 |
| --- | --- |
| A2 authoritative SELF | identity is distinct from mutable state |
| A3 continuity | persisted bytes are evidence for reconstruction, not identity authority |
| Genesis identity separation | organism, runtime, image, checkpoint and execution-state identities must not collapse |
| GVR0 birth / lineage / Resume | birth is one-time; later continuity advances one lineage |
| Store generation chain | accepted successors bind exact predecessors; conflict does not become "latest wins" |
| Work content addressing | immutable realizations may have content identities distinct from logical identity |
| N1 immutable source / revisable interpretation | later review does not rewrite the original source |
| N2 object vs transport identity | a locator or transport realization is not the object identity |
| N3/N4 reading lineage and graph | later readings and graph relations preserve prior identities rather than mutating them |

## Derived model

The research indicates that R0.2 should not model identity as one mutable object with a URL and an `updatedAt` field. It should distinguish at least these layers:

```text
RecordId
  stable logical identity
        |
        v
RecordLineage
  accepted history of the same Record
        |
        +-- generation 0 -> RevisionId A
        +-- generation 1 -> RevisionId B
        `-- generation 2 -> RevisionId C

RecordLocator
  route / slug / filename / external handle
  resolves to RecordId but is not RecordId

RecordRef
  follows the logical Record

PinnedRecordRef
  resolves one exact accepted revision
```

### Birth

A Record begins through a single identity-admission event:

```text
candidate identity
  -> validate namespace and kind
  -> prove identity has never been admitted before
  -> admit generation 0
```

After birth, the identity cannot be born again. A later version is a successor revision, not a new birth of the same identifier.

### Continuity

A successor revision is eligible only when it preserves the same `RecordId`, the same constitutive `RecordKind`, and names the exact accepted predecessor required by the lineage rule.

```text
generation N
  -> candidate N+1
  -> predecessor validation
  -> canonicalization
  -> immutable RevisionId
  -> admission
```

A file overwrite, database update, build output or route change is not sufficient to establish continuity.

### Conflict

Two incompatible candidates that both claim to succeed the same accepted head do not silently collapse into one history. Conflict must remain explicit until an admissible resolution procedure selects or supersedes a lineage branch.

R0.2 therefore must not define continuity as `highest updatedAt wins`, `latest file wins`, `latest commit wins`, or `latest route wins`.

### Tombstone

Deletion from a projection is not erasure of identity. Once admitted, a `RecordId` remains permanently reserved. A tombstone terminates or withdraws the active lineage while retaining enough history to prevent identifier reuse and historical reassignment.

### Locator and alias

A route is a public locator, not ontological identity. A legacy route may become an alias to the same `RecordId`. Once a locator has publicly identified one Record, it must not later resolve to a different Record without an explicit historical boundary.

### Logical versus pinned reference

R0.2 needs two reference semantics:

```text
RecordRef
  -> the logical Record and its current admissible head

PinnedRecordRef
  -> one exact RecordId + RevisionId
```

The first is appropriate for relationships such as "publication is about this system". The second is required when a claim, evidence statement, migration or audit must remain bound to an exact historical state.

## Candidate identity laws

The machine-readable companion `identity-grounding.v0.json` records fifteen non-normative candidate laws, IG-01 through IG-15. They are research inputs to R0.2, not amendments to EC-01 through EC-15.

The strongest candidates are:

- logical identity is not mutable state;
- logical identity is not content identity;
- `RecordKind` is constitutive and immutable after birth;
- identity birth occurs at most once;
- accepted revisions form an explicit predecessor lineage;
- conflicts fail closed rather than resolving by recency alone;
- admitted IDs are never reused, including after tombstone;
- locators and aliases are not identity;
- revision order does not imply epistemic superiority;
- immutable source material is not rewritten by later interpretation;
- references may be logical or revision-pinned;
- representation/storage/framework changes cannot mint identity;
- graph relations do not rewrite the identities of their endpoints.

## What R0.2-PRE establishes

R0.2-PRE establishes a grounded vocabulary and a candidate law set for the next normative cut. It establishes that the future Record contract must separately model logical identity, immutable revision identity, lineage, birth, conflict, locators, aliases, references and tombstones.

## What R0.2-PRE does not establish

R0.2-PRE does **not** establish:

- the final `RecordId` byte/string format;
- the final `RecordKind` enumeration;
- a hashing algorithm for revisions;
- canonical serialization rules;
- the final lifecycle state machine;
- the amendment API;
- route grammar;
- migration of legacy `Project` objects;
- R0.2 completion.

Those become normative only in **R0.2 — Record Identity Contract**.
